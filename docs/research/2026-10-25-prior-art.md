### 1.2 Rule list

Cites are packages/engine/src. Confirmed [high, direct] unless marked. Reproduce exactly; never correct.

1. 28 tiles enumerated a=0..6, b=a..6 (reducer.ts:27-35); use this order as the mask index. Tiles in hands and the boneyard are stored [low, high].
2. Fisher-Yates shuffle, injected rng, default CSPRNG (reducer.ts:39-58).
3. Seats 2v2 n, e, s, w; 1v1 n, s (types.ts:112-114). Seven tiles per seat in seat order; the rest is the boneyard, 14 in 1v1, 0 in 2v2 (reducer.ts:120-133). Teams: 2v2 n/s 0, e/w 1; 1v1 n 0, s 1 (types.ts:116-119).
4. Round 1: the highest double opens (seat scan, strict ">"), so the 6-6 in 2v2. No double (1v1 only): the highest pip-sum tile; ties to the earlier seat, then the first such tile in hand order. Placed automatically as oriented in the hand (reducer.ts:90-109, 135-163, 174), so its lower pip is on the left (Inferred from rule 1).
5. Then currentTurn is the next seat, lastPlayedBy and starterThisRound are the opener, pass counters 0 (reducer.ts:176-184). The automatic tile counts as the opener's play for both bonuses.
6. Later rounds: empty board; the previous round's winning seat (who went out, or the tranque winnerSeat) leads any tile; lastPlayedBy null; scores carry; fresh deal (reducer.ts:482-487, 520-564). startNewRound acts only in phase round_over (reducer.ts:531).
7. Turn order 2v2 n, e, s, w; 1v1 n, s (types.ts:103-110). "To the right" means the next seat.
8. Moves only in phase playing, by the seat on turn (validate.ts:58-63).
9. A play names a tile in hand (either orientation) and end left or right (validate.ts:67-81).
10. Empty board: any tile, either end, placed as oriented in the intent (validate.ts:82-84, reducer.ts:78).
11. Otherwise the tile must contain the chosen end's pip; its other pip becomes the end (validate.ts:85-91, reducer.ts:79-87). A double d-d fits only an end showing d. With ends L != R, the tile L-R gives R|R on the left, L|L on the right.
12. Must play when able: pass and draw are rejected while any tile fits (validate.ts:101-103, 107-110).
13. Draw: 1v1 only, no legal play, non-empty boneyard (validate.ts:94-105).
14. One draw pops tiles from the boneyard's end until the first fitting tile or an empty boneyard (reducer.ts:385-394). The turn stays, pass counters do not change, no score (reducer.ts:396-405).
15. After a draw that found a fit, the last tile drawn is the only legal tile; only the end is a choice. Inferred [high, inference].
16. Pass: only with no legal play, in 1v1 only with an empty boneyard (validate.ts:107-115); advances the turn and increments both pass counters (reducer.ts:408-414, 460-470).
17. Domino: emptying the hand ends the round, checked before the lock, so going out on the locking tile is a domino (reducer.ts:306-316). Points: pips in every hand, partner included, boneyard excluded (scoring.ts:35-43).
18. Capicua: +25 when the domino's closing tile fits both ends as they were before it (reducer.ts:236-240, scoring.ts:91-98). Yes: 3-5 on 3|5, 5-6 on 5|5, 2-2 on 2|2. No: 2-2 on 2|5, empty board, tranque.
19. Tranque: after a play that does not empty the hand, the round ends if no tile outside the board (hands and boneyard) fits either end (reducer.ts:318-322, validate.ts:45-51). Blocker = placer; rival = next seat, always an opponent. Fewer own-hand pips wins for that player's side; equal pips go to starterThisRound (la mano), even when he is neither. Points: all hands, boneyard excluded (scoring.ts:63-82).
20. A 1v1 lock can happen with tiles in the boneyard (reducer.ts:320; reducer.test.ts:314).
21. Only the round-winning side scores (reducer.ts:190-198). Reaching the target sets finished and winnerTeam, else round_over (reducer.ts:206-218). Target default 100 (reducer.ts:119); the Capi API allows 100 or 200.
22. Capicua is round score: not capped, can win the game (reducer.test.ts:1537).
23. Pase corrido (2v2): the 3rd pass since the last play, with the next seat lastPlayedBy (not null), gives that team +25; the round continues and lastPlayedBy plays next; it can repeat (reducer.ts:408-458). Threshold Infinity in 1v1 (reducer.ts:413).
24. Pase de salida (2v2): a non-ending play by the opener's partner, when the board held only the opening tile, lastPlayedBy is starterThisRound, and passesSinceLastPlay is 1, gives the opener's team +25 (reducer.ts:334-373). Every round, the automatic 6-6 included. The partner's pass cancels it; the next pass (by the seat before the opener, the third pass since the opening tile) is then a pase corrido paying 25 once (reducer.ts:425-429; the comment at reducer.ts:423 says "fourth pass", but the code pays on the third).
25. Cap: a mid-round bonus pays only if scores[team] + 25 < target (strict); otherwise the move is plain, no callout (reducer.ts:349, 431).
26. Bonuses and later round points bank in the same column (reducer.test.ts:2021).
27. The next leader is the individual round winner, not the team.
28. Forfeit (120,000 ms silence in live mode, never in turn_based) is an app rule (forfeit.ts:9, 42-78); not modeled.

Rules 15, 17, 19, 20, and 22 are the likeliest to be implemented wrong (rules lane).

### 1.3 The three refinements

1. Only the mid-round bonuses are capped, and strictly (score + 25 < target). Capicua can end the game. Confirmed [high, direct].
2. Boneyard pips never score in 1v1, on a domino or a tranque. Confirmed [high, direct].

### 1.4 Tranque parity and the O(1) lock

- A tranque always shows equal ends X|X with all 7 X tiles on the board, so locked if and only if L == R and popcount(board & suit[L]) == 7. Inferred with a proof [high, inference]: chains are linear (reducer.ts:72-88) and suit X has 8 halves, so an even number of ends show X.
- Measured on Capi's engine: 7,403 of 7,403 tranques in 6,000 seeded games; a board-only test agreed with isBoardLocked on 779,804 states.
- Parra and Trucco 2025, https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.06790, prove it and add: at least 10 board tiles and board pips of at least 6k+42 [high, direct]. Capi's base tranque payout is then at most 126 pips (Inferred).
- The boneyard half of isBoardLocked (validate.ts:49) is redundant, and the lock reveals nothing. Property-test every declared tranque against these facts.

### 1.7 Information model per action

Public: board with orientation, hand sizes, boneyard count, scores, target, starterThisRound, lastPlayedBy, pass count, history. Certain deductions, Inferred [high, inference] (validate.ts:94-115; reducer.ts:135-163, 385-471):
- Round-1 opening: 2v2 reveals only who held the 6-6. In 1v1 a placed double d puts every higher double in the boneyard; no double means all 7 are in the boneyard and the other hand has no higher pip sum.
- A lead or a play reveals the tile only; the end choice for a both-ends tile is a signal, not a certainty.
- A pass (2v2, or 1v1 with an empty boneyard): no tile with L or R, for the rest of the round.
- A 1v1 draw of k tiles: the drawer held no L or R tile; the first k-1 drawn lack L and R; a found fit is played next (forced, so public); with no fit, all k lack L and R.
- Pase corrido: three voids. Round end: pip totals, and the blocker's and rival's pips (reducer.ts:229-234, 256-266). The tranque itself reveals nothing.
- 1v1 voids are time-stamped: track acquisition batches (initial hand, each draw's non-fitting tiles). Model the draw as a chance node over an unordered boneyard.
- So the engine needs an information-set type (own hand, public history, hand sizes, boneyard count, per-seat voids, 1v1 opening deductions), and the sampler must respect all of it. SBEngine states the same draw constraint, https://github.com/Mawxy/SBEngine [medium, direct].

### 2.2 Technique notes

- PIMC is the state of the art for bridge (GIB) and Skat (Kermit) [high, direct]. https://arxiv.org/pdf/1106.0669; https://cs.du.edu/~sturtevant/papers/skat.pdf; https://arxiv.org/pdf/1911.07960.
- Solver cost, Lead (simplified rules, one M1 core, 0 mismatches against naive minimax on 550 positions): full 2v2 deal median 7.7 ms, max 44 ms; 0.345 ms at 21 tiles left in hands. 1v1 with a fixed draw order: median 0.46 ms, max 1.09 s, so cap nodes. TT key: four hand masks, canonical ends, mover, pass count, plus the boneyard pointer in 1v1 [medium, direct].
- PIMC pilot, Lead (simplified rules, 500 duplicate pairs): PIMC20 beat a heaviest-tile bot by +21.33 points/round (SE 1.09); an omniscient team beat PIMC20 by +49.24 (SE 0.86) [medium, direct].
- Long et al. 2010: PIMC is strong when leaf correlation is high; in their synthetic trees the worst absolute case is a mid-range disambiguation factor (df), and play improves sharply as df approaches 1; Skat (df about 0.6) sits where PIMC does well. Lead: a 2v2 seat's information set halves about 4.1 times per own turn (df about 0.80 under the lane's own conversion, not directly comparable to Skat's measured 0.6); 1v1 unmeasured, expected lower [medium, analogy]. https://ojs.aaai.org/index.php/AAAI/article/view/7562.
- PIMC weaknesses: strategy fusion, non-locality, no information-gathering or information-hiding plays [high, direct]. In 2v2 the partner is omniscient in every sampled world, so signaling gets zero value [high, inference]. https://eprints.whiterose.ac.uk/id/eprint/75048/1/CowlingPowleyWhitehouse2012.pdf.
- ISMCTS tied determinized UCT in Dou Di Zhu; the POM variant suits 1v1 draws [high, direct]. https://eprints.whiterose.ac.uk/id/eprint/75048/; https://ojs.aaai.org/index.php/AIIDE/article/view/12679; https://ai.dmi.unibas.ch/papers/sievers-helmert-ki2015.pdf.
- alpha-mu: small bridge gains at about 13 times the time; 1v1 endgames only [high, direct]. https://arxiv.org/pdf/1911.07960 (the 13x figure); https://arxiv.org/pdf/2101.12639.
- IIMC and EPIMC: cheap upgrades given fast solves [medium, direct]. https://era.library.ualberta.ca/items/df9f1055-df09-43ed-910b-38da4e9d0792; https://arxiv.org/pdf/1903.09604; https://arxiv.org/pdf/2408.02380.
- Inference weighting gave the largest reported gains over plain PIMC in Skat (Kermit cardplay 996 against 779 points per 36-game series, 1,600 games, Buro et al. 2009, Table 1); ranking it the largest gain is the lane's inference, and for dominoes it is a Lead. Exact 2v2 sampling under voids is a small DP; 1v1 needs a history-consistent sampler [high, direct]. https://arxiv.org/pdf/1905.10911.
- Bridge engines: BEN cannot read defensive signals; NooK is known only from press [medium, direct]. https://content.iospress.com/articles/icga-journal/icg0006; https://github.com/lorserker/ben; https://www.malaymail.com/news/tech/gadgets/2022/04/02/a-next-gen-ai-has-managed-to-beat-several-bridge-world-champions/2051115.
- DDS ideas: bound TT entries, zero-window search, move ordering (TT move, heavy tiles, doubles), cheap bounds, and double-dummy loss shown beside information-set loss in review [high, direct]. https://raw.githubusercontent.com/dds-bridge/dds/develop/doc/Alg-dds_x.pdf; https://raw.githubusercontent.com/dds-bridge/dds/develop/doc/2014-11%20Performance%20and%20Benchmarking.pdf; https://github.com/dds-bridge/dds.
- CFR family: no fit at the opening (1,184,040 private hands against hold'em's 1,326; guarantees only for two-player zero-sum) [high, analogy]. https://arxiv.org/pdf/1701.01724; https://www.ijcai.org/proceedings/2017/772; Pluribus, Science 365:885-890; https://arxiv.org/pdf/2007.13544; https://arxiv.org/pdf/2112.03178.
- 2v2 is an adversarial team game; claim no equilibrium guarantees [medium, analogy]. https://arxiv.org/pdf/1711.06930; https://proceedings.mlr.press/v202/zhang23j.html; https://www.mit.edu/~gfarina/2022/subgame_solving_teams_neurips22.
- Exact endgames below a world-count threshold (for example 1e3) [medium, direct].
- AlphaZero-style learning: low fit as a core, medium as a component inside PIMC or ISMCTS [medium, analogy]. https://iclr.cc/virtual/2022/poster/6832; https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/artificial-intelligence/articles/10.3389/frai.2023.1014561/pdf.
- DouZero, PerfectDou, Suphx: train with perfect information, act with imperfect [medium, analogy]. https://arxiv.org/abs/2106.06135; https://arxiv.org/abs/2203.16406; https://arxiv.org/abs/2003.13590.
- NNUE: train small info-set nets on search outputs; skip a 2v2 perfect-information value net [high, analogy]. https://stockfishchess.org/blog/2020/introducing-nnue-evaluation/; https://www.chessprogramming.org/NNUE.
- Partnership: SPARTA, BAD, Off-Belief Learning; evaluate by cross-play [medium, analogy]. https://ojs.aaai.org/index.php/AAAI/article/view/6208; Foerster et al., ICML 2019; https://proceedings.mlr.press/v139/hu21c.html.
- Perft: complete-line counts per deal with divide output, generated from Capi [high, analogy]. https://www.chessprogramming.org/Perft.
- 1v1: sample hand and draw order jointly; compare against ISMCTS with partially observable draws [medium, inference].
- Domino algorithm prior art is thin [medium, direct]. https://cacmb4.acm.org/magazines/1973/8/11845-a-learning-program-which-plays-partnership-dominoes/abstract; https://scholar.afit.edu/etd/617; https://www.infona.pl/resource/bwmeta1.element.ieee-art-000006855868.

### 2.3 Measurement method

- Unit: a duplicate pair (same deal, seats swapped; 1v1 same boneyard order). SPRT with stated bounds, alpha = beta = 0.05, in points/round or match-win probability; Elo only beside a CI. Fishtest example bounds: gainer [0, 2], non-regression [-1.75, 0.25] [high, analogy]. https://github.com/official-stockfish/fishtest/wiki/Creating-my-first-test; https://github.com/official-stockfish/fishtest/wiki/Fishtest-faq; https://www.chessprogramming.org/Sequential_Probability_Ratio_Test.
- Variance, Lead (simplified 2v2): round-margin SD 27.4 to 32.2 points (37.8 with PIMC20); duplicates cut games needed only 1.08 to 1.65 times, deal-feature control variates 1.05 to 1.06 [medium, direct]. A 1 point/round edge at 2 SE with SD 28 needs about 3,136 rounds; 0.5 needs about 12,500.
- AIVAT-style control variates from the engine's own values [medium, direct]. https://arxiv.org/abs/1612.06915; https://www.martin.zinkevich.org/publications/competition.pdf.
- Per version: margins against a heuristic bot and the previous version, and the omniscient gap.
- Objective (orchestrator resolution): round points first, then a match equity table W(my score, their score, target, who leads next) with the bonus cap [medium, analogy]. https://www.gnu.org/software/gnubg/manual/html_node/Match-Winning-Chance.html; https://bkgm.com/articles/Kazaross/RockwellKazarossMET/index.html.
- Calibration: Brier score and reliability bins on held-out games.
- Humans: NooK-style duplicate, humans and engine on the same deals against the same fixed bots; state the claim's scope [medium, analogy]. https://singularityhub.com/2022/04/03/a-hybrid-ai-just-beat-eight-world-champions-at-bridge-and-explained-how-it-did-it/; https://lesswrong.com/posts/yHxmJch8dJoH6dwwz.
- Inference quality: TSSR = p(true state | history) x number of states. Endgame error: PIMC loss against exact solutions.

## 3. Domino prior art

### 3.1 Upstream verdict

No public engine implements Dominican rules with search and measured strength, and no domino AI has measured results against elite humans. The public bar is "beats a greedy heuristic" [high, direct; closed commercial engines cannot be inspected; searched GitHub, PyPI, npm, crates.io, arXiv, OpenAlex, app stores]. Build from scratch with capi/packages/engine as the only rules oracle. Perfect-information dominoes is PSPACE-complete competitively for generalized sets, but a double-six determinization stays small [high, direct]. https://erikdemaine.org/papers/Dominoes_FUN2014/.

### 3.5 Strategy concepts as features and tests

[medium, direct] unless marked. Capi pays the winning side all pips in all hands, so do not import "shed heavy tiles" from losers-only scoring (https://www.pagat.com/domino/line/partnership.html; https://acento.com.do/cultura/domino-9463164.html); let search find pot management [medium, inference].

## 5. Long-running agent practices

Adapted to the standalone repo. [high, direct] unless marked.
1. Save the prompt in the repo (docs/prompts/BUILD.md, CONTINUE.md); chat-only instructions can be lost at compaction, CLAUDE.md is re-read.
2. Two prompts: the first session builds state files, scripts, and an end-to-end skeleton; each later session reads state, runs the regression check, finishes one feature, verifies, commits, updates progress.
3. Feature list as JSON, all entries failing at first; change only the pass flag and evidence, after the verify command passes in the current session; never delete, reword, merge, or reorder entries.
4. Each session starts with git log, progress, features, the fast check, and a replay of one fixed 1v1 and one fixed 2v2 game; a regressed feature goes back to failing and is fixed first.
5. One feature at a time, green at every commit; commit before context runs low.
6. Verification is external and every progress claim cites a tool result from this session. In Park and Choi's pilot the agent claimed improvement in all 54 cycles; the measured change was zero or negative in 56%.
7. Never loosen a test, threshold, or game count, and never change seeds after seeing a result.
8. A known-good oracle (Carlini used GCC): Capi's engine through golden traces and, with CAPI_DIR set, a lockstep fuzzer; a mismatch prints its seed.
9. Agent-readable output: short summaries, logs to files, one ERROR line per failure with its seed, a --fast fixed-seed mode.
10. "Bench: <nodes>" in every search or eval commit; refactors keep it [medium, analogy]. Record each side's exact configuration and confirm it took effect; an illegal move, crash, or timeout blocks a strength claim (TeXCCChess, Coda) [high, analogy].
11. Exit conditions are commands with passing output; after two attempts without progress, stop and ask. Done means a command the owner can run that prints the best move and win probability.
12. Fresh-context review at milestone boundaries only, scoped to correctness. Owner oracles are the acceptance tests; no stub counts as passing.
13. Do not stop for context length or end a turn on a plan; batch stop-and-ask questions and keep working. Tell compaction to keep the feature id, files changed, commands with seeds, last bench, open failures, and owner decisions word for word.
14. CLAUDE.md under 200 lines, each rule a check; plain imperatives with short reasons, no capitals or CRITICAL.
15. Smallest end-to-end version first; follow-ups go in the progress file. State goals, constraints, and checks, and leave method choice open [medium, direct].
16. Subagents do not see the pasted prompt (Explore and Plan skip CLAUDE.md), so restate hard constraints. Parallel agents only on independent work. Long matches run in the main session's background shell.
17. Ideas from other engines, never code; dependencies, vendored code, and the license need owner approval [medium, analogy].
18. Follow the section order of capi/docs/prompts/polish-pass-1.1.md.
19. Owner rules: durable identifiers are his; layers; simplest code; no shims; done means observed; reports at permanent docs paths; no em dashes; "what's next" split by who acts; ship-audit before release; small commits to main without a Co-Authored-By trailer (nothing pushed until he decides on a remote).
20. Session reports lead with the outcome and end with what only the owner must do; features sized to one session; long runs report wall-clock time and game counts [medium, direct].
