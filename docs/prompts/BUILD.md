- Confirmed (capi-rules-spec[1]), the rules most likely to be implemented wrong: pase corrido and pase de salida exist only in 2v2 and are the only capped payments, strictly (they pay only if score + 25 < target). Capicua is not capped and can end the game (algorithms[22] says otherwise; follow Capi). Boneyard pips never score in 1v1. Going out on the locking tile is a domino. A tranque tie goes to the round's opener even when he was not compared. A 1v1 lock can happen with tiles in the boneyard. The salida applies to the automatic 6-6; the partner's pass cancels it, and the next pass (by the seat before the opener, the third pass since the opening tile) pays one pase corrido (Capi's comment at reducer.ts:423 says "fourth pass"; the code pays on the third, reducer.ts:425-429). The next leader is the individual round winner. Inferred: after a 1v1 draw that finds a fit, only the last tile drawn is legal.
- Confirmed (capi-rules-spec[3], domino-prior-art[11]): the M1e tranque geometry (proved by Parra and Trucco 2025, Prop. 2.1 and 2.2; equal ends with the full suit held on all 7,403 tranques in 6,000 seeded Capi games, while the 10-tile and 6k+42 bounds were not measured). Inferred (refining algorithms[11], which lists seat rotation as exact without this caveat): seat rotation is exact only with the opener and automatic tile fixed, because the 1v1 tie-break uses seat order; pip relabeling changes scores and the opener, so it is used only in line-count tests.
- Confirmed (arithmetic) and Measured (capi-rules-spec[9]): one 2v2 seat sees 399,072,960 consistent worlds at a free lead and 133,024,320 as a round-1 non-opener; over 300 random Capi-rule games the median was 7,350 at 13 board tiles and 22 at 20. A 1v1 opponent hand has at most C(21,7) = 116,280 options before boneyard order. A seat void in s numbers can hold only (7-s)(8-s)/2 tiles. Worked example (domino-prior-art[13]): 4-1, 4-3, and 4-5 on the board, 4-4 and 4-2 in your hand, partner void in 4s, one opponent void in 6s: the other opponent holds 4-6.

Verification standard
- Check every claim in a report, progress.md, REPORT.md, the README, or a commit message against a command output from this session or a committed result with its command. Otherwise write "not verified". Report failures with their output.
- A rule is verified only by parity with Capi, never by a test written from your own reading; the solver by naive minimax and table on and off agreement; a sampler by exact enumeration, never-peeks, and the fallback counter.
- Strength claim format: both sides' exact settings (commit, Bench, budgets, worlds, seeds, threads), mode and target, held-out duplicate pairs, mean margin in points per round with 95% CI, game-win rate with 95% CI, SPRT bounds and outcome (H1, H0, or inconclusive; a run that hits its maximum pair count is inconclusive, reported with its CI), raw beside corrected once M5 exists, wall time, peak RSS, command, result file. - Win probabilities are judged on held-out games never used to build the table or train a net.
- Performance: the Bench node count shows whether search behavior changed; equal counts mean the same nodes were searched. Timings carry commit, thread count, and uptime load, are measured twice, and are compared only under similar load. A speedup-only change keeps Bench unchanged.
